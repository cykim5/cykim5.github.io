Protocol Agent for Instrument Workflows

LLM agents, Python, instrument protocols, Isayev Lab, CMU

An LLM agent that turns a scientist's plain language description of an experiment into a structured, executable instrument protocol. A researcher states the intent, the measurements and the conditions, and the agent works out the steps and parameters the instrument needs. The protocol stops being a paragraph in a notebook and becomes a precise, repeatable object a machine can run.

It checks requested settings against what the device can actually do before a protocol is final, built for the CLARIOstar plate reader. That check is the core of the project, because a protocol that reads well and fails on the device helps nobody.

Two-Axis Tilt Stage for Opentrons

Hardware control, Opentrons, Python, lab automation

Loading the model

Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom. These are the printed parts, shown one at a time in the orientation they are printed in.

Custom motion hardware for an Opentrons liquid handling robot. The stage tilts a plate along two axes partway through a run, so liquid pools to one side of each well at an angle a fixed deck position cannot reach. Because it is driven from protocol code, tilting becomes an ordinary step inside an automated workflow, called the same way pipetting or incubation is.

Designed and built with Raspberry Pi motor control for CMU lab automation coursework. It covers the whole device integration path: mechanical design that fits the robot deck, motor control, and the software interface that lets a protocol drive the hardware repeatably. Two stepper driven cams sit under a platform pivoting on a fixed ball, giving tilt in any direction up to 17 degrees.

The geometry is a parametric CAD model rather than a hand drawing, so dimensions regenerate from a parameter block. Before anything was printed I simulated 25 motor position combinations and checked every one for collisions and for the platform staying seated.

Gene-ius

LLM agents, knowledge graphs, Python, team of 5

An AI agent that helps laboratories interpret genetic variants of uncertain significance, which account for roughly a third of variants in ClinVar. Across 23 retina and heart genes it ranks which variants to test first, then answers whether a variant is harmful, how it breaks the protein, which disease follows, and what treatment strategy might fit. That ranking hands a lab a short, evidence backed list to take into physical testing.

Evaluated with a time machine design: the model sees only data available in January 2023 and is graded on variants experts resolved afterwards, so no variant is ever evidence for itself. It found 38 later proven disease variants in its top 500, against 18 for AlphaMissense.

Production Data Platform

Python, PostgreSQL, AWS, AI agents, RLWRLD

A production data platform I built from scratch at RLWRLD, a robotics company in Seoul, to turn robot training footage into structured, model ready data. It replaced annotation the team had been doing by hand in spreadsheets, logging worker movements every ten seconds. Pipelines carry each recording through annotation, quality control and analytics, so the team can see which tasks have enough data and which still need more.

In production it cut three hours of manual work per hour of footage to under thirty minutes of review, and a team of ten was using it daily by the end of my internship. It became the data layer the robot foundation model training depended on.

Cancer Screening Cost-Effectiveness Model

Python, Monte Carlo simulation, SEER data, health economics, team project

A simulation that asks whether annual multi-cancer early detection screening is worth its cost, and for whom. These tests screen for many cancers from a single blood draw. We built an individual based Monte Carlo simulation that follows simulated people month by month and compares two worlds: usual care, and usual care plus annual screening. I built the lung cancer model. Each world runs separately for a lower risk and a higher risk group, because a test can be a poor buy on average and a good buy for people at high risk.

Cancer incidence, stage at diagnosis and survival come from SEER data, with test sensitivity by stage, treatment costs and quality of life weights layered on top. The model totals cost and QALYs in each world, where one QALY is a year of life in full health. The headline number is the ICER, the extra dollars spent for each extra QALY gained, reported by risk group, checked against the published results of Raoof et al. (2025), and stress tested to show which assumptions move the answer.

KIVO

Conversational AI agents, product, CMU Hack a Startup finalist, team of 3

A conversational AI agent layer for physical spaces, starting with hotels and airports. It picks up at a front desk, on an in room TV, or at an airport gate, and carries context between them, so a hotel can offer a personal service without adding front desk load and a traveler can get an answer without hunting through apps.

Built with Alice Kim and Umang Pandey for CMU Hack a Startup, where KIVO reached the top 15. We ran customer discovery with hotel, airport and retail operators, and our scoring of those interviews put hotels first, which is where the pitch focuses.

MeowBox

Market strategy, user research, business model, pitch competition winner

A TikTok style mini games product where I led market strategy and research. I sized the market, mapped the competitive landscape and built the business model for a two sided product connecting game creators with casual players. The analysis put the opportunity at $6.7B and gave the team a clear view of which segment to enter first.

It won its pitch competition. It also changed how I start things: earlier product work had taught me what it costs to build before validating demand, so this one began with user research, and the product decisions followed from what we found.
