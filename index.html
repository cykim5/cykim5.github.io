Protocol Agent for Instrument Workflows

LLM agents, Python, instrument protocols, Isayev Lab, CMU

An LLM agent that turns a plain language description of an experiment into a structured, executable instrument protocol, so it becomes something a machine can run rather than a paragraph in a notebook.

In progress at the Isayev Lab at CMU, built for the CLARIOstar plate reader. The hard part is generating a protocol the instrument will actually accept, since its documented control commands can only run protocols that already exist.

Two-Axis Tilt Stage for Opentrons

Hardware control, Opentrons, Python, lab automation

Loading the model

Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom. The assembled view is the device as built, exported from the CAD model. The separate parts are shown in the orientation they print in.

Custom motion hardware for an Opentrons liquid handling robot. The stage tilts a plate along two axes partway through a run, so liquid pools to one side of each well. Because it is driven from protocol code, tilting becomes an ordinary step in an automated workflow, called the same way pipetting is.

Designed and built with Raspberry Pi motor control for CMU lab automation coursework, covering the whole device integration path: mechanical design that fits the robot deck, motor control, and the software interface a protocol drives it through. Two stepper driven cams under a platform on a fixed ball give tilt in any direction up to 17 degrees, checked across 25 motor positions in simulation before anything was printed.

Gene-ius

LLM agents, knowledge graphs, Python, team of 5

An AI agent that helps laboratories interpret genetic variants of uncertain significance, which account for roughly a third of variants in ClinVar. Across 23 retina and heart genes it ranks which variants to test first and explains whether a variant is harmful, how it breaks the protein, and which disease follows. The ranking hands a lab a short, evidence backed list to take into physical testing.

Evaluated with a time machine design: the model sees only data available in January 2023 and is graded on variants experts resolved afterwards, so no variant is ever evidence for itself. It found 38 later proven disease variants in its top 500, against 18 for AlphaMissense.

Production Data Platform

Python, PostgreSQL, AWS, AI agents, RLWRLD

A production data platform I built from scratch at RLWRLD, a robotics company in Seoul, turning robot training footage into structured, model ready data. It replaced annotation the team had been doing by hand in spreadsheets, and its pipelines carry each recording through annotation, quality control and analytics.

It cut three hours of manual work per hour of footage to under thirty minutes of review, and a team of ten was using it daily by the end of my internship. It became the data layer the robot foundation model training depended on.

Cancer Screening Cost-Effectiveness Model

Python, Monte Carlo simulation, SEER data, health economics, team project

A simulation asking whether annual multi-cancer early detection screening is worth its cost, and for whom. It follows simulated people month by month and compares usual care against usual care plus annual screening, run separately for a lower risk and a higher risk group, because a test can be a poor buy on average and a good buy for people at high risk. I built the lung cancer model.

Incidence, stage at diagnosis and survival come from SEER data, with test sensitivity by stage, treatment costs and quality of life weights on top. The headline number is the extra cost per extra year of healthy life gained, reported by risk group and checked against the published results of Raoof et al. (2025).

KIVO

Conversational AI agents, product, CMU Hack a Startup finalist, team of 3

A conversational AI agent layer for physical spaces, starting with hotels and airports. It picks up at a front desk, an in room TV or an airport gate and carries context between them.

Built for CMU Hack a Startup, where KIVO reached the top 15. I ran customer discovery with hotel, airport and retail operators, and my scoring of those interviews put hotels first, which is where the pitch focuses.

Market strategy, user research, business model, pitch competition winner

A mini games platform where games behave like a feed: you swipe into one and play it in a tap without downloading. I led market strategy and research, sizing the market at roughly $160B a year in total, $80B serviceable and $3.6M to $7.2M obtainable, and arguing that the product was not creating a gaming market but entering an existing one through a new distribution layer. Airborne Avian Influenza in Live Bird Markets

R, mixed effects regression, epidemiological data, UC Berkeley

A study of how ventilation in live bird markets in Dhaka and Gazipur, Bangladesh relates to airborne avian influenza. I cleaned the data and ran the statistical analysis in R, produced the figures, and wrote the data analysis section of the paper.

The modelling uses mixed effects regression, which separates differences between markets from variation within them. Published as a preprint on SSRN as LeBoa et al. (2025) and submitted to Building and Environment.

OncoTarget Scout

Python, public multi-omics data, Phases 1, 2, and 3 complete

A target prioritization pipeline for one group of lung adenocarcinoma patients: tumors carrying STK11 or KEAP1 mutations. These tumors respond poorly to immunotherapy and to chemotherapy, carry worse outcomes across treatments, and have no approved therapy aimed at the alteration itself. The pipeline produces a ranked shortlist of candidate genes.

p>

Phase 3 added a single-cell layer from CELLxGENE, 1,953 lung adenocarcinoma cells, and confirmed that 19 of the 20 shortlist genes are active in the malignant cells themselves. A time-split check rebuilt the ranking on DepMap data frozen before 2021: 12 of 20 genes survived the older data, and SPRR1B did not. Each gene now has a plain-language reason card, and the top five have a validation plan naming the cell lines and assays that would confirm or kill them.

A gene has to earn its rank across independent layers of evidence, so that nothing ranks on one signal alone: activity in tumors against healthy lung in TCGA, functional dependency in DepMap CRISPR screens, expression in the malignant cells themselves rather than the immune cells around them, a safety window against vital normal tissue in GTEx, and the link to patient survival. Four of the five layers are in the ranking now. The single-cell layer comes in Phase 3. Three checks are fixed before the ranking runs, including a time split that asks whether the method would have surfaced the KRAS G12C inhibitors before they were approved.

Phase 1 tested the method on the full lung adenocarcinoma cohort from TCGA, 528 tumors and 59 healthy samples, ranking 35,273 genes on expression and survival. Phase 2 asked the subgroup question, splitting the cohort into 143 mutant tumors and 379 wild type, and adding CRISPR dependency data from 40 mutant lung cell lines and a safety check from healthy organ data. The dependency-supported shortlist has 20 genes, led by INTS6 and NFE2L2, the gene that encodes NRF2. The pipeline rediscovered NRF2 on its own, which is the pathway KEAP1 loss activates. Thirteen of the 20 hold up under different weightings.

The work also caught its own errors. Phase 1's top hit turned out to be an artifact of dividing by a near-zero baseline, and Phase 2 fixed it with shrinkage. KRAS, EGFR, and CD274 rank in the middle of the subgroup ranking, which is the correct result. The data shows they are not subgroup-selective targets.
